# Common Cuckoo — Brood Parasitism and Egg Mimicry

The common cuckoo (*Cuculus canorus*) is the vault's paradigm case of **multi-stage deception across a coevolutionary arms race**. The female cuckoo mimics the host species' eggs to avoid rejection; the hatched chick mimics the sound of an entire host brood to maximize feeding; and the adult female mimics the sparrowhawk to suppress host aggression during egg-laying. Each deception exploits a different receiver bias. All three are deceptions operating simultaneously.

## The Three Layers of Deception

### 1. Egg Mimicry (Visual → Host Parent)

Female cuckoos are divided into **gentes** — groups specializing in a particular host species, laying eggs that match that host's eggs in color and pattern. Cuckoos parasitize over 100 host species; the most common UK hosts are dunnock, meadow pipit, and reed warbler.

Key facts:
- Egg matching is imperfect but effective: 28% of cuckoo eggs in one study were described as "almost perfect" mimics
- Hosts reject "poorly mimetic" eggs more often
- Common redstarts, parasitized longer than dunnocks, have evolved better discrimination AND the cuckoo has evolved more accurate mimicry for them; dunnocks, parasitized more recently, still accept obviously different eggs — the arms race is in different phases with different hosts
- Egg appearance is inherited matrilineally (on the W chromosome in females), allowing gentes to maintain different egg types despite male cuckoos mating with multiple female types

The cuckoo maintains a developmental head start: female cuckoos incubate eggs internally for an extra 24 hours before laying, so chicks hatch earlier than host eggs.

### 2. Adult Mimicry of Sparrowhawk (Visual → Host Parent)

The cuckoo's barred underparts and flight silhouette resemble the Eurasian sparrowhawk, a predator of small birds. This mimicry gives the female cuckoo time to access host nests without being mobbed. Experimental evidence: reed warblers treated mounted cuckoo models more aggressively than sparrowhawk models — but still avoided both, and cuckoo mimicry reduces attack enough to allow egg-laying. The two female plumage morphs (grey and rufous) may persist via frequency-dependent selection against the more commonly recognized morph.

### 3. Chick Begging Calls (Acoustic → Host Parent)

The cuckoo chick, once it has evicted all host eggs and nestlings, must elicit enough food for a bird that grows to 3× the host's size. A single chick cannot produce the visual stimulus of a full brood. Solution: the cuckoo chick produces a begging call that sounds "remarkably like a whole brood of host chicks" — stimulating the host parents to provision at near-brood rates. This vocal manipulation is particularly critical when cuckoos are raised alongside host young, where competition would otherwise starve the parasite.

## The Coevolutionary Arms Race

- **Egg stage**: Hosts evolve better discrimination → cuckoo evolves better mimicry → hosts evolve better discrimination... (documented at different phases in different host species)
- **Chick stage**: Hosts have evolved to reject cuckoo eggs but **not cuckoo chicks** — the chick appears after the critical detection window and exploits parental care instincts that are harder to suppress
- **Adult stage**: Host mobbing behavior is the pressure that drove sparrowhawk mimicry; Eurasian sparrowhawk predation itself may be declining, potentially relaxing selection for the mimicry

Edward Jenner — later famous for smallpox vaccination — was elected Fellow of the Royal Society in 1788 for his work on cuckoo biology, specifically documenting the chick's egg-eviction behavior and the anatomical adaptations (a hollow in the chick's back for leverage) that enable it.

## Open Questions

- How do gentes maintain specialization when male cuckoos mate across multiple gentes? (Genetic analysis suggests male contribution is smaller than expected)
- The chick's begging call is described as mimicking a whole host brood — is it specifically tuned to the host species, or is it a generic superstimulus?
- Why haven't hosts evolved to reject cuckoo chicks? Is the recognition window too late, or does the chick exploit an unconditional parental care instinct?
