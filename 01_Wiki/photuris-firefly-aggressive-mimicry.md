# Photuris Firefly — Aggressive Flash Mimicry

Female *Photuris* fireflies (family Lampyridae) mimic the species-specific flash patterns that female *Photinus* fireflies (a related genus) use to attract mates — then eat the *Photinus* males that respond. This is **aggressive mimicry**: the signal exploits a mate-recognition system, converting it into a predation lure. The "femme fatale" firefly is the entomology canonical case.

## What the Signal Is

Fireflies communicate via bioluminescent flash patterns that are species-specific — each species has a distinctive pattern of flash timing, duration, and number. Female *Photinus* fireflies produce a response flash when they see a male's pattern, inviting approach for mating.

Female *Photuris* evolved to produce imitations of *Photinus* female response flashes. A *Photinus* male, seeing what appears to be a conspecific female's response, approaches and is eaten. *Photuris* can mimic the flash responses of multiple *Photinus* species, switching between them — a flexibility that mirrors the [[fork-tailed-drongo-false-alarms]] system of varied mimicry.

## Why This System Exists

*Photinus* males have no way to distinguish a real conspecific female from a *Photuris* mimic using the signal channel they evolved to use. The male's detection system is tuned for mate location, not predator avoidance via the same channel. The deception exploits the fact that the signal's meaning within the species (female availability) is used as a lure by a predator.

This is distinct from the cuckoo or killdeer cases because the deceived party (*Photinus* male) is not the parent but a mate-seeking conspecific of the mimicked species. The deception crosses species entirely.

## Acquisition and Flexibility

The basic flash mimicry appears to be innate — *Photuris* females don't need to observe *Photinus* to produce the imitation. However, the multi-species capability (mimicking different *Photinus* species) suggests either a broader innate repertoire or some flexibility in flash pattern production. This is less well-documented than the drongo's demonstrated learning.

## Connection to Deception Cluster

*Photuris* is the vault's example of **aggressive mimicry** (luring prey) as opposed to:
- **Protective mimicry** ([[mimic-octopus-dynamic-mimicry]]) — deceiving predators
- **Brood parasitism** ([[common-cuckoo-brood-parasitism]]) — deceiving parents
- **Kleptoparasitic mimicry** ([[fork-tailed-drongo-false-alarms]]) — deceiving competitors

All four exploit different receiver biases — predator avoidance, parental care, alarm response, and mate recognition respectively.
